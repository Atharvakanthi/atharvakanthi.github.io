Introduction

This report presents a quantitative profile of YouTube content related to Sakleshpur, a hill station in the Western Ghats region of Karnataka, India (Hassan district). Sakleshpur is an emerging destination for tourism, homestay hospitality, and agricultural investment, particularly in coffee estates and managed farmland. This analysis was conducted to understand the structure, scale, and trajectory of online content and audience engagement around this location.

The dataset covers 7,107 YouTube videos published between November 2011 and August 2026, collected from 429 channels discovered through YouTube search, supplementary web search (including Kannada-script queries), and landmark-based relevance filtering. Of these, 6,486 comments were collected and scored for sentiment. View, like, and comment counts are available for 3,635 videos (51.1% of the total); the remainder have been categorized and tracked but not yet snapshotted for engagement data. All figures in this report derived from view or like counts reflect the 3,635-video subset only.

The central findings are: content production around Sakleshpur underwent a structural shift in 2023, growing over 300% in a single year; the categories still accelerating are commercial (real estate, coffee estate investment) rather than tourism (homestay, trekking, travel vlogs), which have plateaued; and audience sentiment is shifting in composition rather than in attitude, as the comment base moves from enthusiastic travel-vlog reactions toward functional real-estate and coffee-estate inquiries.

Methodology

Data collection. Channels were discovered via the YouTube Data API v3 search endpoint using 12 query terms (English, romanized, and Kannada-script variants including "Sakleshpur," "Sakaleshpura," and "ಸಕಲೇಶ್ಪುರ"). An independent web-search pass was run separately to capture content invisible to YouTube's own search (which has documented non-deterministic result behavior). This web-search pass recovered 30 additional videos and 20 new channels, including a Kannada-language news cluster covering recurring monsoon landslide events on the Sakleshpur railway line that English-only queries had missed entirely. Each video was checked against a relevance vocabulary (location names plus confirmed local landmarks: Manjarabad Fort, Bisle Ghat, Shiradi Ghat, Kaginahare, Bettada Byraveshwara Temple, Charmadi Ghats) before inclusion. Of all videos checked across all discovered channels, approximately 95% were filtered out at the pre-classification stage as clearly irrelevant (large news networks, unrelated travel channels); of the 7,107 that passed, a further 3.5% (248 videos) were classified as off-topic during content analysis.

Classification. Each video was assigned a category and extracted keywords/themes via a multi-provider LLM classification pipeline. The input for each classification was the video's title, description, and up to three sample comments. The pipeline used a cascade of providers (Groq, Cerebras, Google Gemini, NVIDIA, Ollama Cloud) with automatic failover across multiple accounts per provider, batching 15 videos per call. Where two providers returned different categories for the same video, the result was flagged as low-confidence ("thin-flagged"); 3.8% of classifications (269 of 7,107) carry this flag. The 10-category taxonomy was not imposed in advance; it was developed iteratively from the data itself. An initial 8-category pass was expanded to 10 after the first results showed unclassified food/dining and news/current-events content that did not fit any existing category.

Sentiment scoring. Each of the 6,486 collected comments was scored as positive, neutral, negative, or mixed. The primary classifier was Sarvam-105b, a model trained natively on Indian-language text including code-mixed Kannada-English, which resolved 98.8% of comments directly. The remaining comments were scored through the same multi-provider fallback cascade used for classification. Where the primary classifier returned a low-confidence result, a second provider was consulted for cross-verification; if both disagreed, the result was thin-flagged. 29 comments (0.4%) have no final sentiment label.

Growth

Content production around Sakleshpur was minimal through 2022 (365 uploads that year, 931 cumulative). In 2023, uploads increased 302% to 1,468, and have remained near that level through 2024 (1,858) and 2025 (1,815). The 2023 surge was driven primarily by homestay and hospitality content (+464 videos year over year), followed by trekking (+237) and general travel vlogs (+201). However, the categories that drove the initial surge have since plateaued: homestay uploads declined 10% from 2024 to 2025, and trekking declined 47% from 2023 to 2024. The categories still posting year-over-year growth are commercial in nature: real estate (+50% in 2024 to 2025) and coffee estate (+64%). This indicates that the content ecosystem around Sakleshpur is transitioning from a tourism-discovery phase into an investment and commercial-interest phase.

Format

Of the 7,103 videos with parseable duration data, 80.6% are classified as Shorts (183 seconds or under). Long-form videos achieve a higher like-to-view conversion rate than Shorts across every category with sufficient sample size (1.91% median versus 1.22% overall), indicating that long-form content generates more engaged viewership despite lower raw reach. Within long-form content, videos in the 10 to 30 minute range show the highest median view counts (14,532 for 15 to 30 minutes) and like-to-view ratios (3.11%). Videos under 5 minutes in long-form format show lower engagement than Shorts on both metrics; this duration range does not benefit from YouTube's Shorts distribution algorithm and is too brief to sustain the deeper engagement associated with longer content.

Categories

Homestay and hospitality content constitutes 41.7% of all tracked videos (2,964 of 7,107), making it the dominant category by volume. However, its average view count (6,647 per video among those with view data) ranks fifth, below food (34,133), nature (24,020), festivals (12,999), and general travel (7,074). This disparity between supply and engagement suggests content saturation in the homestay category. In 7 of the 10 categories, a single channel accounts for 25% or more of that category's total views. Channels

The view distribution across videos is extremely concentrated: the top 1% of videos (36 of 3,635 with view data) account for 73.9% of all recorded views, and the top 10% account for 93.9%. Haris wanderlust, a general travel channel with 69,900 subscribers, generates 29.5% of all tracked views from just 10 videos in scope, driven primarily by a single video ("Beautiful village in Karnataka", 6,962,408 views). This channel is not Sakleshpur-specific; it covers travel across Karnataka. The bulk of dedicated Sakleshpur content production comes from channels in the 1,000 to 10,000 subscriber range (139 channels, 2,751 videos in scope). Channels above 100,000 subscribers contribute relatively few videos (119 total across 29 channels), consistent with Sakleshpur being an incidental topic for large general-interest creators rather than a sustained focus.

Efficiency

To compare engagement across categories independent of channel size, views were divided by channel subscriber count to produce a per-subscriber reach ratio. Coffee estate content shows the highest median ratio (0.397), meaning a typical coffee estate video reaches an audience equivalent to approximately 40% of the publishing channel's subscriber base. Food (0.357) and general travel (0.348) follow. Homestay content shows the lowest ratio (0.050), but this figure is an artifact of Stone Valley Resorts (16,100 subscribers, 1,820 videos in scope), which contributes 61% of the category's video volume and has a subscriber base nearly four times larger than the median of any other category. Excluding this single channel would materially change the homestay efficiency figure, but the distortion is presented here for transparency rather than silently corrected.

Sentiment

Overall, 58.0% of scored comments are positive, 34.3% neutral, 4.5% negative, and 2.7% mixed. However, the quarterly trend shows positive share declining from approximately 78% in 2021 to 37% in 2025. This decline is primarily compositional rather than attitudinal. In 2021, 64% of all comments were on general travel content, which runs 74% positive by nature (enthusiastic reactions to scenery, trip recommendations). By 2025, over half of comments were on real estate and coffee estate content, where the baseline sentiment is 10% and 20% positive respectively, because comments on that content are predominantly transactional ("price?," "contact number?," "yield is too low"). Within general travel content alone, positive share declined from 88% to 62% over the same period. This is a real but substantially smaller decline than the aggregate figure suggests, and it does not indicate a deterioration in audience perception of Sakleshpur as a destination.

Keywords

Keywords were extracted per video during the classification pass. The terms shown below appear across at least 5 of the 10 categories and no single category accounts for more than 50% of their occurrences, making them the shared vocabulary of the Sakleshpur content space rather than markers of any one content type. "Hassan" (the administrative district) and "western ghats" (the mountain range) function as geographic anchors connecting content across categories. Notably, Sakleshpur is consistently co-mentioned with Coorg (567 co-occurrences across videos) and Chikmagalur (533), indicating that audiences and creators treat these three locations as a connected regional set rather than independent destinations. This has implications for positioning: content and marketing framed around the "Sakleshpur-Coorg-Chikmagalur corridor" aligns with how the audience already thinks about the geography.

Gaps

Plotting video count (a proxy for content supply) against average views (a proxy for audience demand) reveals where the two are mismatched. Food and dining content has the lowest supply in the dataset (48 videos with view data) but the highest average views (34,133). Even after removing the dominant channel in this category (Ashish Vidyarthi Actor Vlogs, 75% of category views), the remaining food videos still average 10,156 views, above the dataset-wide mean. This indicates genuine unmet audience interest rather than a single-outlier artifact. At the opposite end, homestay and hospitality content is the most supplied category (1,148 videos with view data) but ranks fifth in average views (6,647), consistent with content saturation. Trekking shows a similar pattern: high volume (352 videos), lowest average views of any non-off-topic category (1,658).

Real Estate

The real estate and land category comprises 622 videos across the full dataset and is one of only two categories (alongside coffee estate) still growing year over year rather than plateauing, with uploads increasing 50% from 2024 to 2025. villbiz properties produces 42% of videos in this category. The keyword profile is distinctly transactional: the most common terms after location names are "sale" (225 occurrences), "price" (81), "contact" (70), "investment" (53), and "agricultural land" (51). Comment sentiment in this category is the most neutral in the dataset (81.1% neutral, 9.5% positive, 7.8% negative), and the median comment length is the shortest of any category (19 characters), consistent with a functional, inquiry-driven audience rather than one engaging with content for entertainment or information. For anyone positioning in this market: content in this space is consumed as a transaction channel, not a brand-building medium.

Comments

Comments were collected as the top 100 per video ranked by YouTube's own relevance algorithm, not as a complete record; videos with more than 100 comments are undersampled in this dataset. Despite this limitation, the timestamp on each comment provides a direct measure of when engagement occurs relative to upload. Homestay (36.1%) and real estate (32.9%) content show the highest share of comments arriving more than 90 days after the video was published, indicating that audiences discover and engage with this content through search and recommendations well after the initial upload period. Food and dining content shows the opposite pattern (4.3% of comments arriving after 90 days), consistent with a spike-driven engagement model where attention is concentrated in the first days after upload and does not persist. For content categories where evergreen engagement is high, the implication is that older content remains discoverable and commercially relevant over extended periods.

Limitations

Engagement data coverage. View, like, and comment counts are available for 3,635 of 7,107 tracked videos (51.1%). The remaining 3,472 videos have been categorized and tracked but were not snapshotted for engagement data because the snapshotting process runs only on newly discovered videos within each pipeline execution, not on previously known ones. All view, like, and average-view figures in this report are derived from the 3,635-video subset only. This is stated in each relevant section but bears emphasis: aggregate totals (27.2M views, 353K likes) represent a lower bound, not a complete census.

Channel backfill. 364 of 429 tracked channels have been fully walked through their upload history. 65 remain in progress, including Stone Valley Resorts (1,820 of an estimated 3,523 videos enumerated so far). Video counts for these channels will increase on future pipeline runs.

Comment sampling. Comments were collected as the top 100 per video by YouTube's own relevance ranking (via the commentThreads.list endpoint with order=relevance). This is not a random or chronological sample. Videos with more than 100 comments are undersampled, and the selection of which comments are included reflects YouTube's relevance algorithm, not a researcher-chosen criterion.

Shorts classification. Videos are classified as Shorts if their duration is 183 seconds or less. YouTube's official Shorts definition also requires a vertical or square aspect ratio, which was not collected in this dataset. A small number of long-format videos with short durations may be misclassified as Shorts.

Language detection. Automatic language identification reports 57.7% of comments as English and 4.8% as Kannada, with the remaining 37.5% spread across 70+ detected languages. This long tail almost certainly represents misclassification of short, romanized, code-mixed Kannada-English text rather than genuine linguistic diversity. Sentiment classification accuracy on these misdetected comments may be lower than on cleanly identified English or Kannada text.

Single-channel concentration. In 7 of 10 categories, one channel holds between 25% and 91% of the category's total views. All per-category average figures should be interpreted with reference to the concentration table in the Categories section. This report does not silently remove outlier channels from averages; both the inclusive and exclusive figures are provided where relevant.

Dislike data. YouTube removed public dislike counts from its API in December 2021. Third-party estimation services exist but were not used. This report therefore has no measure of negative engagement beyond comment sentiment, which captures a different signal (active written response rather than passive disapproval).
