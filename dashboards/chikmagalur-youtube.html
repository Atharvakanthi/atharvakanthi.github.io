Introduction

This report presents a quantitative profile of YouTube content related to Chikmagalur, a hill town and coffee-growing region in the Western Ghats of Karnataka, India (Chikkamagaluru district). This analysis was conducted to understand the structure, scale, and trajectory of online content and audience engagement around this location.

The dataset covers 7,948 YouTube videos published between September 2013 and August 2026, collected from 648 channels discovered through YouTube search, supplementary web search (including Kannada-script queries), and landmark-based relevance filtering. Of these, 105,088 comments were collected and scored for sentiment. View, like, and comment counts are available for 7,948 videos (100.0% of the total); the remainder have been categorized and tracked but not yet snapshotted for engagement data. All figures in this report derived from view or like counts reflect the 7,948-video subset only.

The central findings are: content production around Chikmagalur underwent a structural shift in 2017, growing 242% in a single year, driven primarily by Homestay and General travel; and no category shows a clear year-over-year growth trend yet.

Methodology

Data collection. Channels were discovered via the YouTube Data API v3 search endpoint using 12 query terms (English, romanized, and Kannada-script variants including "Chikmagalur", "Chikkamagaluru", "ಚಿಕ್ಕಮಗಳೂರು"). Each video was checked against a relevance vocabulary (location names plus confirmed local landmarks: Chikmagalur district, Mullayanagiri, Baba Budangiri, Bababudangiri, Kudremukh, Kemmangundi, Hebbe Falls, Kalhatti Falls, Bhadra Wildlife Sanctuary, Horanadu, Sringeri, Mudigere) before inclusion. Of the 7,948 videos that passed this relevance filter, 3.1% (249 videos) were subsequently classified as off-topic during content analysis.

Classification. Each video was assigned a category and extracted keywords/themes via a multi-provider LLM classification pipeline. The input for each classification was the video's title, description, and up to three sample comments. The pipeline used a cascade of providers (Groq, Cerebras, Google Gemini, NVIDIA, Ollama Cloud) with automatic failover across multiple accounts per provider, batching 15 videos per call. Where two providers returned different categories for the same video, the result was flagged as low-confidence ("thin-flagged"); 5.3% of classifications (425 of 7,948) carry this flag. The 10-category taxonomy was not imposed in advance; it was developed iteratively from the data itself. An initial 8-category pass was expanded to 10 after the first results showed unclassified food/dining and news/current-events content that did not fit any existing category.

Sentiment scoring. Each of the 105,088 collected comments was scored as positive, neutral, negative, or mixed. The primary classifier was Sarvam-105b, a model trained natively on Indian-language text including code-mixed Kannada-English, which resolved 9.5% of comments directly. The remaining comments were scored through the same multi-provider fallback cascade used for classification. Where the primary classifier returned a low-confidence result, a second provider was consulted for cross-verification; if both disagreed, the result was thin-flagged. 470 comments (0.4%) have no final sentiment label.

Growth

Content production around Chikmagalur was modest through 2016 (12 uploads that year, 25 cumulative). In 2017, uploads increased 242% to 41, and have remained near that level since. The 2017 surge was driven primarily by Homestay and General travel content.

Channels

The view distribution across videos is extremely concentrated: the top 1% of videos (79 of 7,948 with view data) account for 56.1% of all recorded views, and the top 10% account for 92.7%. Dr Bro, with 3,050,000 subscribers, generates 27.1% of all tracked views from 65 videos in scope, driven primarily by a single video ("Ramoji Film City Part -1 | Kurukshetra Kannada movie|| Ep.3 | Dr Bro", 2,476,462 views). The bulk of dedicated Chikmagalur content production comes from channels in the 1,000 to 10,000 subscriber range (199 channels, 2,239 videos in scope). Channels above 100,000 subscribers contribute relatively few videos (2361 total across 80 channels), consistent with Chikmagalur being an incidental topic for large general-interest creators rather than a sustained focus.

Efficiency

To compare engagement across categories independent of channel size, views were divided by channel subscriber count to produce a per-subscriber reach ratio. Homestay content shows the highest median ratio (1.48), meaning a typical Homestay video reaches an audience equivalent to approximately that share of the publishing channel's subscriber base. Trekking (0.361) and Coffee estate (0.355) follow. News content shows the lowest ratio (0.006) but this figure is influenced by PUBLIC IMPACT (214,000 subscribers, 1,361 videos in scope), which contributes 94% of the category's video volume. Excluding this single channel would materially change the News efficiency figure, but the distortion is presented here for transparency rather than silently corrected.

Sentiment

Overall, 60.8% of scored comments are positive, 26.5% neutral, 9.7% negative, and 2.6% mixed. However, the yearly trend shows positive share moving from approximately 59.2% in 2017 to 55.1% in 2026. The largest-looking category movements (nature/wildlife, general travel) are concentrated in one or two videos rather than reflecting a broad category-wide pattern, and no other category or pair robustly accounts for the shift -- no dominant compositional or attitudinal driver can be cited with confidence from the data currently available.

Keywords

Keywords were extracted per video during the classification pass. The terms shown below appear across at least 5 of the 10 categories and no single category accounts for more than 50% of their occurrences, making them the shared vocabulary of the Chikmagalur content space rather than markers of any one content type. "Chikkamagaluru district" and "western ghats" (the mountain range) function as geographic anchors connecting content across categories. Notably, Chikmagalur is consistently co-mentioned with Coorg (60 co-occurrences across videos), Sakleshpur (27 co-occurrences across videos), indicating that audiences and creators treat these locations as a connected regional set rather than independent destinations. This has implications for positioning: content and marketing framed around the "Chikmagalur, Coorg and Sakleshpur corridor" aligns with how the audience already thinks about the geography.

Gaps

Plotting video count (a proxy for content supply) against average views (a proxy for audience demand) reveals where the two are mismatched. Food and dining content has the lowest supply in the dataset (138 videos with view data) but the highest average views (119,007). Even after removing the dominant channel in this category (FoodyMonk, 46% of category views), the remaining food videos still average 69,849 views, above the dataset-wide mean. This indicates genuine unmet audience interest rather than a single-outlier artifact. At the opposite end, homestay and hospitality content is the most supplied category (611 videos with view data) but ranks 6 in average views (12,047), consistent with content saturation. Trekking shows a similar pattern: high volume (1,251 videos), lowest average views of any non-off-topic category (14,777).

Real Estate

The real estate and land category comprises 356 videos across the full dataset and is one of the categories still growing year over year rather than plateauing. Green Realtors produces 34% of videos in this category. The keyword profile is distinctly transactional: the most common terms after location names are "mudigere" (95), "chikkamagaluru" (72), "coffee estate" (62), "coffee plantation" (44), "green realtors" (40). Comment sentiment in this category is the most neutral in the dataset (45.6% neutral, 45.3% positive, 5.7% negative), and the median comment length is 26 characters, consistent with a functional, inquiry-driven audience rather than one engaging with content for entertainment or information. For anyone positioning in this market: content in this space is consumed as a transaction channel, not a brand-building medium.

Comments

Comments were collected as the top 100 per video ranked by YouTube's own relevance algorithm, not as a complete record; videos with more than 100 comments are undersampled in this dataset. Despite this limitation, the timestamp on each comment provides a direct measure of when engagement occurs relative to upload. Homestay (25.3%) and real estate (17.7%) content show the highest share of comments arriving more than 90 days after the video was published, indicating that audiences discover and engage with this content through search and recommendations well after the initial upload period. Food and dining content shows the opposite pattern (26.9% of comments arriving after 90 days), consistent with a spike-driven engagement model where attention is concentrated in the first days after upload and does not persist. For content categories where evergreen engagement is high, the implication is that older content remains discoverable and commercially relevant over extended periods.

Limitations

Engagement data coverage. View, like, and comment counts are available for 7,948 of 7,948 tracked videos (100.0%). The remaining 0 videos have been categorized and tracked but were not snapshotted for engagement data because the snapshotting process runs only on newly discovered videos within each pipeline execution, not on previously known ones. All view, like, and average-view figures in this report are derived from the 7,948-video subset only. This is stated in each relevant section but bears emphasis: aggregate totals (212.0M views, 4.8M likes) represent a lower bound, not a complete census.

Channel backfill. 585 of 648 tracked channels have been fully walked through their upload history. 63 remain in progress, including PUBLIC IMPACT (1,771 of an estimated 9,432 videos enumerated so far). Video counts for these channels will increase on future pipeline runs.

Comment sampling. Comments were collected as the top 100 per video by YouTube's own relevance ranking (via the commentThreads.list endpoint with order=relevance). This is not a random or chronological sample. Videos with more than 100 comments are undersampled, and the selection of which comments are included reflects YouTube's relevance algorithm, not a researcher-chosen criterion.

Comment coverage. 3,872 of 7,524 relevant videos (51.5%) have at least one comment on record; the remainder have no comments (disabled or genuinely none). All comment-level findings in this report (sentiment, comment timing, evergreen rates) are drawn from this subset, not the full relevant-video population. Of the videos with no comments, 85 have comments confirmed disabled by YouTube's API; the remaining 3,567 were checked but simply have zero comments.

Reply capture. 93,580 top-level comments and 11,508 replies on record (11.0% of all comments are replies). Replies are captured via a full paginated comments.list(parentId=...) walk for every thread with totalReplyCount > 0 -- not a partial/inline-only capture.

Shorts classification. Videos are classified as Shorts if their duration is 183 seconds or less. YouTube's official Shorts definition also requires a vertical or square aspect ratio, which was not collected in this dataset. A small number of long-format videos with short durations may be misclassified as Shorts.

Language detection. Automatic language identification reports 45.4% of comments as English and 12.9% as Kannada, with the remaining 41.7% spread across 92 other detected languages. This long tail almost certainly represents misclassification of short, romanized, code-mixed Kannada-English text rather than genuine linguistic diversity. Sentiment classification accuracy on these misdetected comments may be lower than on cleanly identified English or Kannada text.

Single-channel concentration. In 8 of 10 categories, one channel holds 25% or more of the category's total views. All per-category average figures should be interpreted with reference to the concentration table in the Categories section. This report does not silently remove outlier channels from averages; both the inclusive and exclusive figures are provided where relevant.

Dislike data. YouTube removed public dislike counts from its API in December 2021. Third-party estimation services exist but were not used. This report therefore has no measure of negative engagement beyond comment sentiment, which captures a different signal (active written response rather than passive disapproval).
